# Value Packaging → Offer Economics

This layer converts the value-packaging idea into a falsifiable economic contract.

## Pipeline

Product facts → presentation layers → offer variant → unit economics → controlled exposure → conversion/purchase evidence → incremental contribution → decision.

## What it prevents

- Treating a prettier package as proof of higher value.
- Claiming a price multiplier from visual quality alone.
- Ignoring packaging/creative/traffic costs.
- Calling a test successful without outcome evidence.
- Confusing higher price with higher contribution margin.

## Required measurements

For each baseline/variant pair record:

- product and variant IDs
- sale price
- unit product cost
- incremental presentation cost
- traffic/acquisition cost per order where known
- exposure/sample size
- conversion/purchase outcome
- contribution margin
- test limitations

The deterministic contract exposes a break-even conversion rate: the minimum variant conversion rate required to preserve contribution per visitor.

## Integration targets

### EASY
Use Product DNA/Product Integrity as the protected source of truth. Generate presentation variants without changing protected product attributes, then pass each variant through this economics contract before treating it as a commercial experiment.

### MONY
Turn the capability into a sellable service: presentation upgrade + offer page + creative variants + measurement. Revenue claims remain evidence-gated by actual commercial outcomes.

### Elite / ARMY-14
Use the contract as a verification boundary: a visual artifact is not a business result. The agent must distinguish generated output, experiment exposure, measured outcome, and recognized revenue.

## Evidence standard

A design hypothesis can be shipped as an experiment without proving a lift. A measured-lift claim requires independent outcome evidence and a non-empty sample. No universal price multiplier is assumed.

Recent 2026 research continues to find associations between packaging design and willingness to pay, but the magnitude is context-dependent. For example, a 612-person premium-tea study reported a positive packaging-design/WTP association, while other recent packaging research tested different categories and methods. These are benchmark signals, not a guaranteed multiplier for any product.
